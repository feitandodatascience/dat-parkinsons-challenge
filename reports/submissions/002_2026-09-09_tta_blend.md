`WARNING: line filtered` and a disqualification warning attached; all three contained
`len(submission)`. This run has **no filtered lines at all**. `Loaded submission format` with no
count, `Still running, 15 min elapsed` instead of a progress denominator, and
`No fallback predictions were needed` instead of a failure count are the three rewrites, and all

**2. Every shipped asset loaded.** 5 CNN checkpoints on `cuda`, 5 lightgbm boosters, `stack.json`,
and the vendored `datpd_vendored/` tree. `Loaded 5 fold models` and `Loaded 5 boosters` are the
two lines that would have caught a staging failure, and both are present. The blend was genuinely
active — this is not a silent fall-through to the CNN alone.

**3. No fallbacks fired.** Every scan was predicted by the real path. Preprocessing held on the
whole test set, at whatever field-of-view range it contains.

**4. Runtime margin is enormous: 15 minutes against 180.** A **12× margin**, measured on the real
test set and the real A100 rather than extrapolated from local Docker. Note that model load
finished at 17:52:29 and the run ended at 18:07:42, so essentially the entire 15 minutes is
per-scan work.

This last point substantially closes the Phase 4 checklist item "extrapolate timing to the full
test set and confirm margin inside 3 hours" — with better evidence than the local test would have
produced. It also de-risks a higher-resolution build: 128³ @ 1.5 mm is 2.37× the voxels, so even
assuming the whole 15 minutes scales linearly (it will not — some of it is fixed cost) the run
lands near 35 min, still a 5× margin.

## The score is better than predicted, and that needs reading carefully

| | OOF | LB | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Run 1 | 0.3186 | 0.3210 | **+0.0024** |
| Submission 2 | 0.3061 | 0.2973 | **−0.0088** |

Two things happened at once, and only one of them is the method.

**The measured gain under-predicted the realised gain.** The paired OOF comparison said −0.01562
(3.1σ, CI [−0.0255, −0.0057]). The leaderboard moved 0.3210 → 0.2973, a gain of **−0.0237** —
about 1.5× what was measured, and outside the upper end of that CI.

**But AUROC moved the same direction, which is the tell.** OOF AUROC was 0.9375; LB AUROC came
back 0.9396. If the extra gain were the blend calibrating better on test than on OOF, log loss
would improve while AUROC stood still. Both improved. That is the signature of a test set that is
simply **a slightly easier draw for this model**, not of the method transferring better than
measured.

**So do not revise the method's value upward.** The honest reading is that the blend + TTA is worth
about the −0.0156 that was measured, and the remaining ~−0.008 is the test set. The CV↔LB gap is
now two observations, +0.0024 and −0.0088, which bounds it at roughly **±0.01** rather than the
±0.0024 a single point suggested. That is still small enough to decide Phase 3 questions on OOF
alone — which is what matters — but it is four times looser than the number `log.md` has been
quoting, and any future decision resting on a margin under 0.01 should be treated as unresolved
rather than won.

The standard error on the leaderboard score itself is unknown and deliberately stays that way: it
depends on the test set's size, which is exactly the quantity that must never be derived or
recorded (§3.1).
